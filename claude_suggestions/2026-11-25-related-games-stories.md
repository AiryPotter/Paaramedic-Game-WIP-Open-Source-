- Sources are web searches on 2026-11-25: store pages, reviews, wikis and research papers. Wikis are fan-written. Where sources disagreed, that is noted. **Spoilers are kept light,** except where a premise depends on them.

## EMS and ambulance games

### Ambulance Life: A Paramedic Simulator (Aesir Interactive / Nacon, 2025)
- **Premise:** A new recruit to the paramedic service in the fictional Pelican City, on the US west coast, sent on calls across four neighborhoods.
- **Structure:** Shifts of 15 to 45 minutes, with calls arriving over the radio. Players listen to patients, assess and treat them.
- **Story:** Almost none. One review sums up the premise as being a new recruit, "and that's about it", and wished for more story. Another review was critical of the technical quality.
- **Telling:** Through the job itself: dispatch calls, patient talk and shift results.

### Neo Harbor Rescue Squad (BancyCo, 2024)
- **Premise:** A new paramedic called only "Rookie" joins an underdog rescue squad in the shadow of the city's well-known Fire Department. The goal is to earn respect and rebuild the squad's reputation.
- **Characters:** Captain Queenie leads the squad. Betty Woo, a TV reporter, uses her show to turn the public against the squad.
- **Inspiration:** Real paramedic stories from Asia.
- **Telling:** A visual novel, with arcade-style minigames for treating patients.
- **Reception:** Split. Some reviewers liked the light humor and characters; others found them clichéd and the story tedious. Reviewers agreed the minigames were easy and the weakest part. Short, with little replay value.

### 911 Paramedic (classic)
- **Story:** No main plot. Moving from EMT trainee to paramedic gives a personal career arc.

### Virtual Responder (BreakAway Games)
- **Premise:** Play through an EMT's career, using EMT skills in high-pressure situations. Closer to training than entertainment.

### Smaller projects
- **Ramping Rush: Ambulance Rescue!** An indie game with narrative design credits, about a busy paramedic in Australia. Sessions are about half an hour.
- **Med Day.** A student project: a text story that walks through a field call and explains the player's mistakes.

## Dispatch games

### 911 Operator (Jutsu Games, 2017)
- **Premise:** An emergency dispatcher takes calls and sends police, fire and ambulance units.
- **Story:** A campaign of shifts in six cities' dispatch centers, including New York, San Francisco and Chicago. No detailed plot was found.

### 112 Operator (Jutsu Games, 2022)
- **Story:** The campaign was rebuilt around a story, with objectives and emails. The developer mentions hearing a frightening murder story, coaching a caller through CPR, and dealing with prank callers. A career mode follows rising through the ranks in a European city.

### Dispatch (AdHoc Studio, 2025)
- **Premise:** Robert Robertson, the hero Mecha Man, loses his mech suit fighting his enemy. He takes a desk job at the Superhero Dispatch Network, sending reformed villains on calls. In return, the network promises to repair his suit.
- **Main arc:** His investigation into his father's death at the hands of Shroud, leader of the Red Ring. Later episodes deal with the Mecha Man family tradition, and Shroud's belief that evil can only be controlled, not stopped.
- **Telling:** Episodic, with choices. Gameplay alternates between story scenes and choosing which hero to send on each call.
- Sources disagree on some episode details.

## Surgeon Simulator

### Surgeon Simulator (Bossa Studios, 2013)
- **Premise:** Nigel Burke, a new surgeon at a fictional hospital (sources say the town of Barnardshire, 1987), operates on a patient named Bob.
- **Story:** Barely any. The game is about clumsy controls: the mouse moves the hand and keys bend each finger. The comedy comes from failing.
- **Origin:** Made in under 48 hours at the 2013 Global Game Jam. It had sold 2 million copies by early 2015.

### Surgeon Simulator 2 (Bossa Studios, 2020)
- **Premise:** Uncover the truth behind a mysterious medical facility. The campaign was written by Rhianna Pratchett.
- **Structure:** 11 missions, about 3 to 4 hours, playable with up to four players.
- **Telling:** Voiceover while the player meets more and more absurd situations. Reviewers compared its dark comedy to Portal. Bob returns, with new background story built around him.

## Medical and hospital games

### Trauma Center series (Atlus, 2005 onward)
- **Premise (Under the Knife):** Derek Stiles, a young surgeon who chose medicine after his father died of an illness, starts his residency at Hope Hospital. He has a rare ability, the "Healing Touch".
- **Main threat:** GUILT, a man-made parasite spread by the terrorist group Delphi. After Derek operates on a GUILT patient, Caduceus, a secret medical agency, recruits him.
- **Under the Knife 2:** Three years later, Delphi and GUILT return, alongside Derek's self-doubt and corporate manipulation.
- **Telling:** Story scenes between surgery stages. The tone mixes hospital melodrama with science fiction.

### Trauma Team (Atlus, 2010)
- **Premise:** Six main characters from different parts of medicine: general surgeon, diagnostician, EMT, orthopedic surgeon, endoscopy specialist and medical examiner. Together they face a deadly infection called "Rosalia".
- **Structure:** Each character has their own episodes that interweave. A seventh chapter unites all six. The EMT story is the series' only playable prehospital role.
- **Characters:** Include CR-S01, a surgeon with amnesia who is serving a prison sentence.
- **Tone:** Reviewers list ghosts, psychic powers, superheroes, a serial killer and wisecracking AI robots. Some reviewers called the story weak.
- Sources disagree on the hospital's location.

### Two Point Hospital (Two Point Studios, 2018) and Theme Hospital (Bullfrog, 1997)
- **Story:** None. The campaign is a series of hospitals in Two Point County, each with its own challenge.
- **Tone:** Comedy from made-up illnesses and silly treatment machines. Some of Theme Hospital's creators made Two Point Hospital.

### Project Hospital (Oxymoron Games, 2018)
- **Story:** No story found. A realistic hospital management game, with departments (for example infectious diseases and traumatology) added as expansions.

## Story-driven dramas

### Pathologic 2 (Ice-Pick Lodge, 2019)
- **Premise:** Twelve days in a remote steppe town during a deadly plague. The player is the Haruspex, Artemy Burakh, a surgeon born in the town.
- **Main arc:** He comes home to find his father dead and himself blamed. He must find the plague's cause and stop it, or fail, while dealing with the town's factions and history.
- **Telling:** Survival with limited time and supplies, so not everyone can be saved. The town is already failing when the story opens. Several endings exist.
- A remake of the 2005 Pathologic.

### That Dragon, Cancer (Numinous Games, 2016)
- **Premise:** Ryan and Amy Green made it about their son Joel, who had brain cancer from infancy and died in 2014. Amy wrote the script; Ryan led design and narrates.
- **Telling:** About two hours, moving between hospital rooms and dreamlike scenes. The title comes from a story the family told of Joel as a knight fighting a dragon named Cancer.
- **Design note from critics:** The player cannot win. The game shows hope, setbacks and loss, and the family mostly follows medical advice they cannot control. One review called it closer to a diary than a game.
- **Reception:** Praised for emotional force. Some found the delivery uneven, and the arcade-style section out of place.

## Serious and training games

### Zero Hour: America's Medic (George Washington University, Virtual Heroes, 2008)
- **Purpose:** Mass casualty training for EMS, mostly paramedics. Made with a US Department of Homeland Security grant by EMS operations, education and research staff.
- **Scenarios:** Sources agree on a few national disaster scenarios, but disagree on how many. One source lists a SARS-like pandemic, an earthquake, a train derailment with a chemical leak, and a sports stadium explosion.
- **Structure:** Scenarios get harder and less controllable as they go on, and can be replayed. Players review their own performance. It was also meant to recruit people into EMS. Now free (PC).

### Pulse!! (BreakAway Games, Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi)
- **Purpose:** Clinical training in a virtual hospital: respond to emergencies, diagnose and treat as in real life.
- **Design principles:** Real-time feedback, repeated practice, safe environment, individual learning, clear outcomes, educational validity.
- **Results:** One review found it more effective than paper-based learning and highly engaging.

### Triage Trainer (TruSim)
- **Purpose:** Major incident triage. Players check casualties through icons (for example breathing or pulse checks), open airways and tag priority.
- **Results:** In a controlled trial, tagging accuracy was higher than with a card-sorting exercise, but triage time was no faster. No longer available.

### Full Code and Body Interact (current virtual patient apps)
- **Full Code:** A mobile medical simulation app with hundreds of cases. The vendor claims about 20 prehospital/EMS cases. A 2024 trial in Singapore compared it with manikin training.
- **Body Interact:** Virtual patients with a physiology engine. Patients can get worse and go into cardiac arrest if problems are not treated. The vendor says it is matched to US and Canadian curricula.
- **Story:** Each case is a short patient story. There is no overall plot. Case counts are vendor claims.

## Common patterns across these games

These patterns are observed, not recommended.

- **Simulators tell little story.** Ambulance Life, 911 Operator, Project Hospital and Two Point Hospital rely on the job itself. Reviewers of Ambulance Life wished for more story.
- **Story-heavy medical games often add fantasy.** Trauma Center has a man-made parasite and the Healing Touch. Trauma Team has a deadly infection and superhero elements. Dispatch uses superheroes.
- **A new-recruit hero is common:** Ambulance Life, Neo Harbor Rescue Squad, 911 Paramedic, Virtual Responder, Trauma Center and Surgeon Simulator.
- **A personal loss often drives the hero:** Derek Stiles (father's illness), Artemy Burakh (father's death), Robert Robertson (father's death).
- **Underdog teams and public image:** Neo Harbor Rescue Squad's squad versus a hostile reporter. Dispatch's team of reformed villains.
- **Several connected stories:** Trauma Team links six specialties, including an EMT, into one story.
- **Serious games use scenarios, not plots.** Zero Hour, Pulse!!, Triage Trainer, Full Code and Body Interact build on cases that get harder, with feedback and replay.
- **Grief and failure as content:** That Dragon, Cancer and Pathologic 2 do not let the player save everyone, and both are remembered for it.
- **Comedy is the other common route:** Surgeon Simulator, Two Point Hospital and Neo Harbor Rescue Squad.
